DIG 123 · Agile Methodologies for Innovation · Class 7

Denning's laws, Conway's Law, CI, CD and DevOps, the innovation triangle, the 3E's, discovery and delivery, Lean Startup, the MVP, the Business Model Canvas, technical debt and system design

This hub collects the flash-cards and the self-check questions of today's four lessons: how the way an organization works decides the product and the service its customers receive (Denning's three laws, Conway's Law, the two-pizza team, Scrum's path to the digital workplace, the value map and the innovation triangle); team execution with the course's 3E's — Empower, Experiment, Evolve — and product discovery next to product delivery; Lean Startup's Build–Measure–Learn loop, the riskiest assumption, the hypothesis that can fail, the decision rule, persevere or pivot, and the nine blocks of the Business Model Canvas; and adaptation: technical debt, components, interfaces, and what a design decision does to WIP, cycle time and lead time. The same shapes return in Lab 04 and in the Mid-Term, with other products and other numbers.

Before you start

The words of this class come from a few free, public sources: the Scrum Guide 2020 (cross-functional, self-managing, the Sprint Review), Amazon's 2015 Letter to Shareholders (Type 1 and Type 2 decisions), the Lean Startup principles (Build–Measure–Learn, validated learning, the MVP (Minimum Viable Product)), Strategyzer's Business Model Canvas and Martin Fowler's Technical Debt Quadrant. When two answers look plausible, choose the one that follows the lecture's rules: the method sets how often users can be heard; a sentence about a team passes only if it names an action and carries a date, an event or an owner; a hypothesis must be able to fail; the decision rule is written before the test; a value proposition states a gain, not a feature; a coupled design makes large cards, and large cards make long lead times.
