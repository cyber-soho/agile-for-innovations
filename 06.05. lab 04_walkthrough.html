Step 0 · Planning — the template, the goal, the hats

Team Studio · 0:00 – 0:1010'Header and footer

What you do

  • The Facilitator reads the D2 vision sentence and the Sprint 2 Goal aloud, once.
  • The Scribe writes today's goal at the top of the template and gives out the four hats — Facilitator, Timekeeper, Scribe, Presenter; the PO (Product Owner, member 3) and the SM (Scrum Master, member 4) keep their Sprint 2 accountabilities.
  • The Class 6 flow table is pasted into Box 1 as it stands, and the three E sentences from the lecture into Box 3. Nothing is rewritten yet.
  • The Timekeeper sets 30 minutes for Step 1.

Done when: The goal is on the page, everyone knows their hat, and the Class 6 material is in front of the team.

Footnote — as of end-October 2026. The template (DIG123_Lab04_D6D7_Template.md) is plain text: opened in any editor it shows the box labels in order. A shared document works as well — keep the box order, graders read top to bottom.

Step 1a · The flow table — three dates, two durations

Team Studio · 0:10 – 0:2212'Box 1

What you do

  • Write the convention first: day = difference of dates (Monday → Friday = 4); started = entered In progress; finished = all five Definition of Done checks; committed = entered Ready.
  • One row per Sprint 1 story, eight at most: points, committed, started, finished, lead time (finished − committed), cycle time (finished − started); for a story still open on October 25, its age (October 25 − started).
  • Under the table: the average cycle time of the finished stories and the longest; the story carried into Sprint 2 keeps its start date.

Done when: Every finished story has two durations, every open one has an age, and two rows recomputed by hand agree with the table.

Footnote — as of end-October 2026. Spreadsheets: in Google Sheets and LibreOffice Calc, subtracting one date cell from another gives the number of days; if the result appears as a date, set the cell format to Number. Menu names and icons differ between versions (checked October 2026); the arithmetic does not.

Step 1b · Daily WIP (Work In Progress) counts and throughput

Team Studio · 0:22 – 0:308'Box 2

What you do

  • Throughput per week: the count of stories finished in the week of October 12 and in the week of October 19; then the total for the fourteen days, divided by 14 for a per-day figure. A count of cards, not of points.
  • Average WIP: the fourteen daily counts of cards in progress — from your Daily Scrum lines and board, or rebuilt from the dates (a card counts on day d if started ≤ d and finished after d or still open) — their sum, and the sum ÷ 14. Note the peak day.

Done when: Two weekly numbers and a per-day throughput; fourteen counts with their sum and average.

Footnote — as of end-October 2026. The workbook in the Class 6 companion ZIP rebuilds the daily counts from the dates with a COUNTIFS formula. A newer spreadsheet release may draw the formula bar differently; the function is unchanged.

Step 1c · The Little's Law line — predicted, observed, one reason

Team Studio · 0:30 – 0:366'Box 2

What you do

  • Predicted average cycle time = average WIP ÷ throughput per day (items ÷ items per day = days).
  • Observed = the average cycle time of the finished stories, from Box 1.
  • Write the two numbers side by side and one sentence on the gap, naming which condition of the law was not met over your window. If the numbers agree, say whether you trust an agreement built on a handful of cards.

Done when: Two numbers and one reason, with matching units — and nothing adjusted to make them agree.

Footnote — as of end-October 2026. Definitions follow the Kanban Guide, edition 2025.5 (May 2025, kanbanguides.org); a later edition may renumber its sections — the flow form of the law does not change. The five conditions are Daniel Vacanti's, from Actionable Agile Metrics for Predictability (2015).

Step 1d · The limit on the column, the method line, the board screenshot

Team Studio · 0:36 – 0:404'Box 2 + screenshot

What you do

  • One line for the limit: the number, the scope (which column), since when, who counts at the Daily Scrum — and the reason taken from your numbers (how many cards were open at the peak, how many finished).
  • One line from your Class 6 decision memo: Scrum, Kanban or Scrumban since October 26, because …
  • A screenshot of your Sprint 2 board with the limits and the policies visible, placed next to Box 2 or on the last page.

Done when: A limit with a number, an owner and a reason; a method with a reason; one screenshot.

Footnote — as of end-October 2026. The board drawn here is generic. Trello, Jira, GitHub Projects, Focalboard and Taiga show a column limit with different badges and menus, and each of them changes its interface several times a year; search the tool's help for column limit, WIP limit or card limit, and screenshot what your tool shows — or photograph the wall.

Step 2 · The product in its business — the value map and the 3E's

Team Studio · 0:40 – 1:0020'Box 3

What you do

  • Four boxes and the arrows between them: who builds the product (your team, and anyone whose data or work you depend on, by role); who uses it (the users of your D2); who pays for it or carries its cost (an organization or a role — or "nobody yet", written honestly); what flows along each arrow (data, money, time, attention, a legal duty met). Label every arrow.
  • An empty box is a finding: write "unknown — to ask [whom] by [date]".
  • Then the three E sentences — Empower, Experiment, Evolve (this course's framing) — each naming something the team does, with a date, an event or an owner.

Done when: Four boxes with labelled arrows and at most one "unknown" with a name and a date; three sentences that name actions.

Footnote — as of end-October 2026. Any drawing tool — Excalidraw, draw.io, tldraw — or paper and a camera. The regulation named in Team Menu's map (Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011) applies to campuses in the European Union (EU); other countries have their own food-information rules — check which applies to yours.

Step 3 · Build · Measure · Learn — assumption, MVP (Minimum Viable Product), hypothesis, metric

Team Studio · 1:00 – 1:2525'Box 4

What you do

  • The riskiest assumption: the belief about the users' behaviour that, if false, makes the product pointless. Each member writes one alone; the PO picks one and says why.
  • The MVP in three lines: what it is, what it is not (three features it deliberately lacks), where and when it runs (November 3 to 7 at most). If it needs more than a day to build, cut.
  • The hypothesis, word by word: We believe that [segment] will [behaviour] because [reason]. We will know we are right when we see [metric] reach [number] by [date]. Then the metric alone: counted what, how, by whom, when, and the number that means "right" — counts only, never names or e-mails.
  • One pivot candidate, decided before the test: if the number is not reached, on November 8 we consider a change of channel, of segment or of value proposition.

Done when: One assumption about behaviour; an MVP with its three absences; a hypothesis with six parts; a counting method without personal data; one pivot candidate.

Footnote — as of end-October 2026. QR (Quick Response) code generators and short-link services change their free tiers often: test the code on two phones the day before, and read what the service stores — counts only is the rule. The hypothesis form is adapted from Jeff Gothelf and Josh Seiden, Lean UX (2013), with a reason, a number and a date added.

Step 4 · Business Model Canvas v0 — five blocks

Team Studio · 1:25 – 1:4520'Box 5

What you do

  • Draw the nine blocks — a printed canvas, a board, or a 3 × 3 table — and fill five: Customer Segments, Value Propositions, Channels, Customer Relationships, Revenue Streams; one to three short lines each.
  • Leave the other four drawn but empty, marked "v1 — Studio 2, December 7".
  • Four checks: every segment is a D2 user or a payer named by role, and the segment of your hypothesis is among them · each Value Proposition is a gain, not a feature · Channels and Relationships exist now or this week · Revenue Streams says who pays, how and roughly how much — or "cost carried by …", as a fact.

Done when: Five blocks filled, four marked for v1, four checks passed.

Footnote — as of end-October 2026. The official template is a free download from strategyzer.com (page and terms checked October 2026; both may change — keep the attribution line when you reuse the file). Online boards such as Miro or Canvanizer have free tiers with limits on boards, members or exports; read them first. Sticky notes on an A3 print, photographed, are accepted.

Step 5 · The adaptation line — components, one what-if, the effect on flow

Home Sprint · 0:00 – 0:2020'Box 6

What you do

  • Name the three to five components your product is made of (a paper prototype has them too: the screens, the data behind them, who updates what).
  • One realistic change request, in a stakeholder's words.
  • Which components change, which stay untouched — and the effect on flow in one sentence: how many cards, how large, what that does to your cycle times and your WIP limit, with the numbers of Box 1 as the scale.

Done when: Components named; one what-if; a flow sentence with a card count and a size.

Footnote — as of end-October 2026. If you draw the components as a Mermaid flowchart, read the live syntax page first — the syntax changes between releases, and a block that rendered in September may need a comma in November. Martin Fowler's Monolith First (2015) is the short background reading.

Step 6 · Sprint 2 Review and Retrospective — five lines

Home Sprint · 0:20 – 0:35 (events held Nov 7–8)15'Box 7

What you do

  • Hold both events at the end of the Sprint (about 10 minutes each; online is fine; invite the user or stakeholder you named at the week plan).
  • Five lines: goal and hypothesis result, persevere or pivot · numbers — real velocity, Sprint 2 throughput, highest WIP seen and whether the limit held, Little's Law over the 28 days of Sprint 1 + Sprint 2 · the voice of the user, in your words · one change for Sprint 3 with an owner, checked at the Sprint 3 Planning on November 23, and the Lab 03 debt repaid or open · one thing to keep.

Done when: Five dated lines with the metric's result, the velocity, the throughput, the limit's record and the recomputed law.

Footnote — as of end-October 2026. Any video tool for a remote Review; the chart in the animation is drawn by hand — a spreadsheet bar chart or a photograph of graph paper is fine. A user who attends appears as "a student" or "the manager", never by name.

Tools are open

  • Every tool and technique in these animations is a starting point, never a rule: swap any of them for anything suitable and available, or build your own — a spreadsheet that computes the three clocks, a script that rebuilds the daily counts, a canvas on a whiteboard, a visit counter you write yourselves.
  • Research before you adopt (ten minutes per tool): official site only · free-tier limits and export · terms and your data (§6.6, §14) · check the link with VirusTotal and Google Safe Browsing (Annex C) · try it five minutes as a team. Name your tools in the footer of your page.
  • Starting points (official sites, checked October 2026): Google Sheets · LibreOffice · Trello · GitHub Projects · Focalboard · Taiga · Excalidraw · draw.io · Mermaid · Strategyzer canvas · Miro · Canvanizer · tutor.com (writing help, free through Blackboard).
